# ADR-0031 — Ranks hub: client-side Overall, Final boards, official entries, visibility, daily replays

Date: 2026-11-13 · Status: accepted, amended 2026-11-14 (decisions 12, 23, 24: user-check rulings F1, F18, F20, F21) · Source: `.scratch/leaderboard-hub/spec.md` (A6, A7, A10–A22, A15, A27, A28, A33, A36, A39) · Amends none. Relates to ADR-0024 (daily boards and the −2…+1 window, unchanged), ADR-0027 (owner table already amended by ticket 03), ADR-0029 (cloud space), ADR-0030 (replay envelope and `LoadSolveReplay`)

## Context
The leaderboards are per game and per today or yesterday only. A player has no single place to see where they placed across games, over a week or a month, or on a past day, and cannot control whether others can challenge or watch them. History took the Ranks tab's slot in the first place, but the hub replaces it; its calendar, stats and replay sections move in with it.

Three facts shape the design. Firestore has no aggregation without Cloud Functions, which are out of scope (the Blaze plan is not in use). A daily board is immutable once its day has passed the write window, so it can be read once and kept. A solve outside the submit window is never posted, so it can only be shown to its owner.

## Decision
1. **Overall is computed on the client from per-day boards.** Pure functions in `core:domain` do the math: `rankBoard(entries, order)` returns rows with rank, N and points; `overall(boards in window)` returns standings with place, total, games and the truncated and incomplete flags; `unofficialPlace(score, board, order)` returns a place. They are deterministic for any device zone and are pinned by the `OverallCases` contract table in `core:testing`, beside `RankOrderCases`.
2. **Points.** `rank = 1 + entries strictly better` (competition ranking; tied entries share a rank and its points). `N` is the number of entries on the board. A solve scores `100 × (N − rank + 1) / N`. Overall is the sum over the window. Equal totals share a place, and the display tie-break is games descending, then uid. There are no per-game weights, no minimum games and no all-time window. Volume counts by design.
3. **Cap at 100.** A board is read in full up to `BoardPage.PAGE_SIZE` (100). A board at the cap costs one `count()` read for N, cached once the board is final. A player outside the top 100 shows "100+". Their solve scores the floor of place 101, `100 × (N − 100) / N`. That is a lower bound with no cliff at 100. It counts as played, and their total shows as "≥". This replaces the 0-points reading of the 03 handoff case 11, whose expectation becomes 33.333.
4. **Partial, never final.** A board that cannot be loaded (offline with nothing cached, or an error) adds nothing and is not counted as played. The Overall result is then **Partial (k of n boards)** and is never shown as final. A solve queued but not yet accepted counts as missing until it lands.
5. **Windows use puzzle-day labels**, not the device zone or the submit date. Week is Mon–Sun containing the anchor day. Month is the anchor's calendar month. The current week or month is clipped at today. Every day in the window is queried. A Final board that comes back empty (for example before a game's boards existed) is stored as an empty board (N = 0) and is never re-read.
6. **Per-game Week and Month boards rank by average over days played.** Each player is ranked by their average over the days they played in the window (caption "Average over days played"; score text such as `avg 4:12`, Strands `0.7 hints · avg 3:05`), ordered by that game's `RankOrder` applied to the averages. Ties share a place. Day boards are unchanged. Overall keeps the sum of percentile points (decision 2).
7. **Final boards are fetched once and stored apart from the main database.** A Final board is a daily board whose day is before the device UTC date minus 3 (one day wider than the rules window, to absorb clock skew). It is fetched once (a one-shot read, plus `count()` when capped), stored in `final_boards.db`, and never listened to again. `final_boards.db` is its own Room database (version 1, destructive schema changes, `FinalBoardDatabase`), excluded from backup and device transfer like `linkedin.db`. The main database stays at v13. Open boards (at most 4 days per game) keep live listeners. Final boards hold no LinkedIn ghost data.
8. **Stores and seams.** `FinalBoardStore` is an interface in `core:data` with a Room adapter (`RoomFinalBoardStore`) and an in-memory fake, so the seam is real. The leaderboard repository gains a one-shot fetch and a `count()` read for final boards. One domain use case, `ObserveRankWindow`, observes the boards of a window for a game set and returns per-board state (Loading, Loaded, Unavailable). It reads final boards from the store and open boards from the existing listener. Overall, per-game boards and title-menu places all derive from that one flow, so switching boards costs no reads.
9. **Read budget (provable with a counting fake).** Reads are listeners plus one-shot fetches plus `count()` calls. An Overall day open is at most 5 board listeners, plus one `count()` per open board at the cap. A week or month open is at most (open days in the window × 5) listeners, plus one-shot reads only for final boards not yet stored. A second open of the same month costs the open boards only. Listeners are scoped to the screen and stop when it is not visible, so rotation and background/foreground re-read nothing. Leaving and returning within a session reuses them. Board loading recovers on the network-online edge and on pull-to-refresh, with one shared backoff for the whole window.
10. **Official is the client submit window.** An entry is **official** when it was posted inside `GameCalendar.inSubmitWindow(game, day)`, the client mirror of the server window −2…+1 server UTC (ADR-0024, unchanged). A solve outside the window is never submitted. Every entry the server board accepted is official, including a LinkedIn sync that lands in the window.
11. **Unofficial is device-only.** A local solve or LinkedIn result for a day whose window has passed is **unofficial**. It is shown only to its owner, as a marked row at `1 + official entries strictly better` (`unofficialPlace`). It has no points, no `games`, and no effect on anyone's place. Nothing is backfilled to the server, and ADR-0024 stands. "Official" is honor-system: the client sets `completedAtMs`. That is a stated non-goal. UI copy says "unofficial", never "late", because "Late" is a duel term in GLOSSARY.
12. **Connections are on Friends boards only.** *(Amended 2026-11-14, F21; replaces the first reading, in which ghosts stayed on per-game day boards and had no row in Overall.)* The LinkedIn connections saved on the phone (ghosts) take places on every Friends board: Overall and each game's Day, Week and Month. They take friend places (decision 23), earn points and add to N there. A connection with a hidden time ranks last on a day board (shared place, `time hidden`). Global boards hold no connection. A connection's synthetic entry is built on the device at read time. It is never stored (not in the Final board store, which keeps only the global top-100 page) and never posted, and no shared document carries it. A local day card can differ from the global board in place, and it is labelled "with LinkedIn connections" when connections are present.
13. **Visibility ladder.** A player's tier is one of `hidden | board | duel | replays`, and each tier includes the ones below it. The default is `board` (today's behaviour), and a missing setting reads as `board`. `hidden` stops future solves from posting to boards. Entries already posted stay, because they are immutable and the name is permanent. Hidden is not retroactive, and the tier copy says so.
14. **Tier storage and seam.** The tier lives in its own per-player doc, `visibility/{uid}`, with a closed key set (`tier`, `v`). Any signed-in player can read it, and only the owner can write it. It is read **on tap only** (Challenge or Watch), never per row. The owner's tier is also kept locally, so posting can honor `hidden` offline. `VisibilityRepository` in `core:data` observes the own tier, sets it, and reads another player's tier once. It has a Firestore adapter (`FirestoreVisibilityRepository`) and a fake that defaults to `board`. The submit watcher consults the own tier for `hidden`.
15. **Challenge is gated on the client.** A row's tier must be at least `duel`. Match-create rules stay unchanged, because rematches also set an invite target and a rules gate would break rematches with default-tier players. `CreateMatch` takes an explicit invite target that flows to the existing draft invite field. An unwanted invite is declined in Duels.
16. **Daily replays.** When the owner's tier is `replays` at solve time, the solve's replay is published to its own doc, `leaderboards/{boardId}/replays/{uid}`. It is at most 12 KiB, the same envelope as duel legs (ADR-0030, `SolveReplay.MAX_BYTES`), and it never rides on the entry. A read needs a signed-in player and the owner's tier equal to `replays` at read time, checked by one `get()`. Lowering the tier therefore revokes reads at once. Create and delete are owner-only, and there is no update. Opt-in covers future solves only. Retroactive publishing is out of scope.
17. **Spoiler gate.** Watch on a row needs the viewer's own completed solve of that same puzzle (game and day), checked on the device. Otherwise the action shows locked with "Solve it first". A row whose owner's tier is below `replays` shows Watch locked with "Private".
18. **Entry hardening.** Daily entries keep `formatVersion` 1 with no new fields (A25). New writes must match a closed key set, and their size is capped. Old entries still read. The rules, the rules-matrix rows and the leaderboard rule-block SHA pins ship together (ticket 25).
19. **Another player's replay opens through `ReplayKey`.** A new `player` kind on `ReplayKey` names the board's day and the other player's uid. Keys carry ids only, never bytes. `LoadSolveReplay` (ADR-0030, decision 9) stays the one replay entry.
20. **Module home.** The hub lives in `feature:history:impl`, as History's successor (ADR-0027 §10). The app picks the Ranks entry, label and icon when `ReleaseFlag.LeaderboardHub` is on, and the History entry otherwise. The feature never reads the flag.
21. **Extension.** Boards come from a list of daily games, so a new daily game appears in the swipe and in Overall with no hub change. Windows, scopes and tiers are sealed types (`RankWindow`, `RankScope`, `VisibilityTier`). There is no other seam. A second scoring rule would trigger a refactor ticket.
22. **Dark until flipped.** The hub ships behind `ReleaseFlag.LeaderboardHub` (`releaseDefault = false`). With the flag off, the app is exactly today's History tab and game-page board. The rules change and the flag flip merge only after the user approves them. The Final board cache is a new file and needs no migration, so it merges dark.
23. **Friends boards number places among friends.** *(F1, F18, 2026-11-14; replaces the first reading, in which Friends only filtered the global list and place, N and points stayed global.)* The default scope is Friends, and Settings → Ranks switches it between Friends and Global. The hub landing, a tab reselect and every link follow it, and a restored selection keeps its own scope. A Friends board holds the viewer, their friends (duel opponents) and the connections the phone holds (decision 12). Every place, N and percentile point on it is computed among those players only, on the board, in the hero, the dock and the title menu, and Friends Overall points are the sum of those friend-board points. Global boards are unchanged, and switching scope reads nothing. A Friends board is built from the global top-100 page, so a friend ranked past global #100 is not on it (A39).
24. **Entry points follow the scope.** *(F20, 2026-11-14.)* The game-page summary row, the Result rank chip and Today's rank row read the place and count in the player's default scope: among friends and connections on Friends, the whole board on Global. With `ReleaseFlag.LeaderboardHub` off they read the whole board, as shipped.

## Consequences
- An Overall open costs at most 5 listeners per day in the window, plus `count()` on capped boards. Final boards are read once per device and then cost nothing.
- Overall is honor-system: `completedAtMs` is client-asserted, so a cheater can post an official entry. Cheat-proof official status is out of scope.
- "100+" and "≥" appear whenever a board is truncated. Partial results are never shown as final.
- A second device zone yields the same standings, because windows use puzzle-day labels and the math is pure.
- Reads of another player's replay depend on that player's tier at read time, so a lowered tier revokes access without a republish.
- `final_boards.db` is a second Room database. It is destructive on schema change and excluded from backup, so a reinstall refetches it.
- Adding a game gains no hub code; the daily game list is the only input.

## Rejected
- **Server-side Overall** (Cloud Functions or a Blaze-plan aggregation): out of scope; the client already holds the per-day boards.
- **All-time windows, per-game weights, minimum games**: the spec keeps volume as the signal by design.
- **Exact ranks past 100**: the floor bound avoids a read per board and a cliff at 100.
- **Final boards in the main database**: would move the main database off v13 and back up a cache; the separate file is excluded from backup.
- **A new ranks module**: would have to extract History's calendar and stats into a core module and leave two near-duplicate tab owners during the dark period (A27).
- **Per-row tier reads**: the tier is read on tap only (A18), which keeps the board read budget flat.
- **Retroactive hiding of posted entries or names, and retroactive replay publishing**: entries are immutable and the name is permanent.
- **Backfilling unofficial solves to the server**: ADR-0024 stands; unofficial results stay on the device.
- **A friend graph or follow feature**: friends are opponents from your matches plus LinkedIn connections (A6, A32).
- **The word "late" for unofficial solves**: it collides with the duel term "Late / LocalOnly".
